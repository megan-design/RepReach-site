I'm Overwhelmed

How Do I Stop Feeling Overwhelmed by the News?

News overwhelm is real, it's designed, and there's a way out that doesn't require you to stop caring.

Why the news feels overwhelming

The News Cycle Is Designed to Feel Overwhelming

News media — traditional and social — is optimized for engagement. The most engaging content is content that produces strong emotional reactions: fear, outrage, urgency. The algorithmic systems that determine what you see prioritize the most alarming content because it gets the most clicks, shares, and time-on-platform.

This means that the experience of following political news is artificially more distressing than the actual state of affairs warrants. Not because the issues aren't serious — many of them genuinely are — but because the way they're presented maximizes anxiety rather than clarity or agency.

Understanding this is the first step to managing it. You're not being unreasonably sensitive. You're responding normally to a system that's deliberately calibrated to produce exactly how you feel.

The core distinction: News that informs you enough to act is valuable. News that makes you more anxious without enabling any action is mostly just stressful. Learning to distinguish between them — and limiting the second category — is the key skill.

Practical changes

How to Change Your Relationship With Political News

Turn off push notifications for news apps

Push notifications are designed to pull you back into the anxiety loop repeatedly throughout the day. Turning them off doesn't make you less informed — it lets you choose when to engage with news rather than being pulled in reactively every time something happens. You can check news on your own schedule, intentionally, rather than responding to alerts.

Distinguish between news and action

For every piece of political news that upsets you, ask: is there something I can do about this right now? If yes — open RepReach, find the relevant issue, contact your representative. If no — acknowledge the information and move on. Sitting with distressing information that you can't act on is what produces anxiety without agency.

Use a curated source instead of a feed

Algorithmic news feeds are designed to keep you scrolling indefinitely. Curated sources — a newsletter, a specific publication's digest, or RepReach's legislation feed — give you what you need to know without the infinite scroll that characterizes most doom scrolling. Choose a source, read it once, close it.

Schedule news time

Check news at specific times — morning and evening, for example — rather than continuously throughout the day. This creates clear on/off periods rather than a background hum of political anxiety that follows you through the day.

The reframe

From Passive Consumer to Active Participant

The overwhelm of political news comes partly from the powerlessness of passive consumption. When you read about problems without having any way to act on them, the information accumulates as anxiety without any corresponding sense of agency.

The shift from consumer to participant changes this dynamic. When you know that your response to alarming political news is to open RepReach and contact your representative, the news becomes something you respond to rather than something that happens to you. That shift from passive to active is one of the most effective treatments for news overwhelm.
