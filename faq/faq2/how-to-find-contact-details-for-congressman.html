How Can I Find Contact Details for My Congressman?

Your congressman's contact information is public — but it's scattered. Here's exactly where to find it.

The official sources

Where Your Congressman's Contact Information Lives

Every member of the U.S. House of Representatives maintains an official website on the house.gov domain. These websites include Washington D.C. office phone numbers, local district office phone numbers and addresses, and a web contact form for constituent email correspondence. This information is public and freely available.

Step 1: Go to house.gov and use the "Find Your Representative" ZIP code tool to identify your congressman.

Step 2: Click their name to go to their official website.

Step 3: Find the "Contact" section for phone numbers, office addresses, and the web contact form.

Or: Enter your ZIP code in RepReach and all contact information appears instantly — no navigation required.

What contact information is available

What You'll Find

Washington D.C. office phone number

The main line for your congressman's Washington D.C. office. This is where legislative staff work when Congress is in session. Calls here are counted and reported to the congressman regarding constituent opinion on legislation.

District office phone numbers and addresses

Most representatives maintain one or more district offices in their home district. These offices are particularly active when Congress is in recess. You can call them with the same constituent contact as the D.C. office, and they are often more accessible for in-person visits.

Web contact form

Most congressional offices use a web contact form rather than a published email address. This is standard — your message goes to the same constituent correspondence inbox. You'll typically need to provide your name, address, and ZIP code to confirm you're a constituent before submitting your message.

Social media accounts

Most representatives maintain official social media accounts. These are less effective for direct constituent contact but can be useful for public expressions of opinion that other constituents can see and amplify.
