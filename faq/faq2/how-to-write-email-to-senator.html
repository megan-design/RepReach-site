How Do I Write an Email to My Senator?

An effective email to your senator takes about five minutes and is far more impactful than a form email or petition signature. Here is exactly how to do it.

Why personalized email beats form email

Your Own Words Carry More Weight Than a Template

Congressional offices distinguish between organized campaign emails — where hundreds of people submit the same pre-written message — and genuine constituent correspondence. Form emails and petition signatures are counted, but they're weighted less than personal messages because they require no individual effort and may not reflect actual constituent opinion.

A personalized email in your own words — even a short one — signals that you took the time to write specifically about this issue. That distinguishes it from mass-campaign contact and carries meaningfully more weight with staff who review constituent correspondence.

Email template:

Subject: [Your City] Constituent Regarding [Issue Name]

Dear Senator [Last Name],

My name is [Your Name] and I am a constituent from [Your City, State]. I am writing to [express my opposition to / express my support for] [specific bill name or issue].

[1–2 sentences explaining why this issue matters to you personally.]

I urge you to [vote no on / vote yes on / take action on] [bill or issue]. Thank you for your service and for representing the people of [your state].

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Your City, State]
[Optional: Your Phone Number]

Key principles

What Makes a Senator's Office Take Notice

Keep it under 200 words

Shorter emails are more likely to be read in full and summarized accurately by the staff member reviewing constituent mail. A concise, clear message communicates more effectively than a long, detailed argument. You are not writing a policy brief — you are registering a constituent position.

Be specific about the issue

Name the bill by its common name or number if you know it. "The reconciliation bill," "S. 1234," "the proposed cuts to Medicaid" — specificity helps the staffer categorize your contact correctly and ensures your email is counted in the right tally.

Include a personal reason

One or two sentences about why this issue affects you personally — "I have a child with a disability who relies on Medicaid" or "I'm a nurse and this bill would affect my patients" — makes your email more memorable and more likely to be flagged as particularly meaningful constituent contact.

End with a clear ask

What do you want your senator to do? Vote yes, vote no, cosponsor the bill, oppose an appointment. Be direct. The clearer your ask, the more useful your contact is to the office tracking it.

Where to send it

Finding Your Senator's Contact Form

Both U.S. senators have official websites at senate.gov with a contact form for constituent correspondence. You can find your senators by going to senate.gov and selecting your state. Most senators use a web contact form rather than a direct email address — this is normal and your message goes to the same place.
