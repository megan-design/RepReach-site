What Do I Say When I Call My Congressman?

Most people hang up before they dial because they don't know what to say. Here is the exact script — and it takes under 30 seconds.

The short answer

You Need Four Things — Nothing More

A constituent call to a congressional office is not a debate. You are not trying to change anyone's mind in the room. You are adding a data point to a tally that will be reported to your representative. That means brevity and clarity are your friends. You need four things:

The complete script:

"Hi, my name is [your name] and I'm a constituent from [your city]. I'm calling to [oppose / support] [specific bill or issue]. I'd like Representative/Senator [name] to [vote no / vote yes / take action]. Thank you."

That's it. The entire call takes 20–30 seconds.

Breaking it down

Why Each Part Matters

Your name

You don't need to give your full name — a first name and last name is enough. Some people prefer to remain anonymous, which is fine, but giving your name makes the contact more credible and more likely to be noted as a genuine constituent contact rather than an organized campaign call.

"I'm a constituent from [city]"

This is the single most important thing you say. Congressional offices represent specific districts and states. A call from a constituent — someone who can vote for or against this representative — is politically meaningful. A call from someone outside the district carries essentially no weight. Always establish that you are a constituent.

The specific issue

Name the bill, the program, or the issue as specifically as you can. "The reconciliation bill," "the proposed Medicaid cuts," "the vote on judicial nominations" — the more specific, the more useful the data point. General calls about being unhappy with the political situation are tallied differently than issue-specific contacts.

Your position and your ask

Support or oppose. Vote yes or vote no. The cleaner and more direct your ask, the more effectively it gets recorded. You are not obligated to argue your case — you are registering your position.

What to expect

What Happens on the Other End

A staff member — often a young staffer or intern — will answer the phone. They will listen politely. They will not argue with you or try to change your mind. They will log your name, location, issue, and position. They will say something like "Thank you for calling, I'll pass that along." That's the whole interaction.

You may sometimes reach voicemail. Leave the same information — name, city, issue, position. Voicemails are counted too.

Don't expect a personalized response. You may receive a form letter reply. That's normal and doesn't mean your call wasn't logged.
