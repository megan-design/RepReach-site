How to Contact Your Representative About Congressional Oversight & Separation of Powers

Executive overreach is bypassing Congress on spending, agencies, and law. Here's how to contact your representative to defend congressional authority.

📋 Federal Issue 🔥 High Urgency 🗳️ Democracy 119th Congress

The Issue

What's at Stake

A core principle of American democracy is the separation of powers — Congress makes the law, the executive branch executes it, and the courts interpret it. In 2026, that balance is under significant strain, with executive actions impounding congressionally-appropriated funds, restructuring agencies without legislative authorization, and bypassing established administrative processes.

Congress has historically asserted its authority through oversight hearings, legislation, and the appropriations process. Whether your representative acts to defend congressional prerogatives — or defers to executive action — is a direct result of constituent pressure and political will.

Frame it as a constitutional principle. Separation of powers is a bipartisan value that resonates with members across party lines.
🎯

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Impoundment Control Act and why does it matter?
The Impoundment Control Act of 1974 requires the executive branch to spend funds that Congress has appropriated. It was passed to prevent presidents from unilaterally refusing to spend congressionally-directed money. Current executive actions that freeze or redirect approved funds may violate this law.
Can Congress force the executive branch to follow the law?
Congress has several tools: oversight hearings, legislation clarifying existing requirements, the appropriations process, and ultimately the courts. Constituent pressure is what motivates members to use these tools.
Does calling about separation of powers make a difference?
Yes. This issue has generated bipartisan constituent concern, and members who have faced sustained calls about executive overreach have been more likely to join oversight efforts.
